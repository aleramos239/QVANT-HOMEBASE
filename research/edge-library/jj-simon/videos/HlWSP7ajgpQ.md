## New or sharper rules
- Overnight trades: after his evening trading ("my overnights start at 9:30 PM, when I go to sleep"; his clock, likely Pacific) he sends accounts in the higher-time-frame bias direction with a resting bracket (stop + target + limit orders, OCO), entry when the trend switches (break of structure). Targets = a ladder of older fair prices: e.g. long to the Asia open, then to the 6 PM open, then to last night's consolidation. "Slightly above 50/50, basically no work."
- 09:25 ET volume spike: if you read its direction, get in early and hold through the 09:30 open (or go breakeven), depending on expected value. "Use asymmetry on the open."
- Funded big trade to a LEVEL: 3 NQ long @ 29,503.25, target +$4,485 (~75 pts) at the consolidation (his new fair), stop -$5,025 (~84 pts). So on a funded account that needs a big win he does aim at the fair level, with a wide stop (~1:0.9).
- Evals take the small "too close" trades; the big funded trade waits until price is far from fair.
- New fair = a consolidation that broke structure and continued away.
- Mid-candle entry when price fails to break structure against you.
- Choppy day: displacement / break signals come too late (price is already back), so he uses LIMIT orders toward fair.
- News candles: usually he does not enter on a news candle (weak), except when price is so close to fair that waiting leaves no room.
- "Any time it's above this line [the fair consolidation], just short." The bias line is a simple above / below rule.
- Bias reasons he gave: news candle color (red news = short bias) and reverting the overnight move.
- Layer when it reverts: on a failed day he loses ~3 tries; when it reverts he wins 6-7 layered trades.
- Chop (a red candle right after every green) is bad for his approach (FTMO evals lost).
- Copy trading: he says don't, but in one huge reversion he copy-traded 17 + 22 accounts because he could not place them one by one fast enough.
- After the session: wait for a trend and volume to die down, then trade WITH the trend for extra eval trades.
- EV bookkeeping: an eval risking a third of its drawdown that loses = a third of its EV (~$90 on a $270 eval).
