## Slide: what I look for
1. New York session open, continuation in the first 5 min.
2. Mean reversion after, 5-90 min (09:35 -> 11:00). Stops at 11:00 even if it reverts after.
3. No max number of trades (at least 10 a day in NY AM).
4. Displacement candles.
5. Fair price: continuation (away) and reversion (back).
6. Fair price = the open (the pre-open price, "95% of the time").

## Slide: key things to know
1. Performs better on prop firms (risk management here doesn't work live).
2. Risk management is the MOST important part (he keeps it for his mentorship).
3. Trade a different account based on what you see: pick the prop account whose needed points match the points available back to fair.
4. A+ = break of structure, A = displacement, B = never take.
5. News days: same trades; news is what changes fair price.
6. Scale: more trades, any displacement toward fair.
7. Why: Fair Price Theory - the NY open is the first major intraday reference point for fair auction; early moves are opening flow, liquidity imbalances, stops, hedging, momentum - not a true lasting re-price.

## Rules said here (new or sharper)
- Accounts: "trade each account one at a time", "one account at a time and one trade per account per day. That is the optimal way." Sometimes copies two together; don't copy-trade until ~$20k a month. Copy trading spreads risk thin; he wants risk concentrated on one account, many trades -> evens out. (= the owner's rotation rule.)
- Continuation = opening candle color, enter at the top (market) at once. Body <= 25 pts -> stop 25 / target 38; body > 25 -> stop 50 / target 76 (examples: 40, 43, 44, 50-pt candles). Skip it when a big wick rejects that side and the color disagrees.
- Why 25 / 38 on evals: eval is -$2,000 / +$3,000 = 1:1.5 itself; trades at 1:1.5 fit how the drawdown trails. On live he would target the fair price instead.
- Displacement, "zero discretion" version: the body is bigger than the previous candle's body. Close past the previous wick = preferred (he sometimes takes ones that don't). 2-3 candles combined = discretion. A candle with no real body that went "all four directions" is not one; he likes a candle that opens and goes straight.
- Break of structure needs a CLOSE past the swing, not a wick. "More recently it's been extremely wicky ... no more limits. Now I'm just waiting for a close." (Replaces the limit orders of HlWSP7ajgpQ.)
- Too close: only ~24 pts back to fair for a 38 target = shouldn't take it.
- Layer only when price breaks structure in your favor while the first trade is ~halfway / 75% to target.
- Breakeven only when "absolutely necessary": the opening continuation stalls and breaks structure against you -> out at breakeven (not at +$300, that messes risk), then flip on the break.
- Fair price moves: consolidation, then a break of structure and continuation away -> delete the open, new fair = the most recent consolidation (volume moved it away). Can move again on the next break.
- News: EXPECTED news (red folder on Forex Factory, e.g. 08:30) is priced in -> fair = the pre-news price; revert the whole move. UNEXPECTED news (a Trump tweet) CHANGES fair -> new fair = the consolidation after it. On a tweet the market runs fast one way: send an eval WITH the move ("slightly above break-even").
- News day: trades before 09:30 back to the pre-news price, then the 09:30 continuation toward fair, then reversions.
- Live vs prop: live = one trade below the line, target the fair price, any stop. Prop = split the same bias into many 25 / 38 trades.
