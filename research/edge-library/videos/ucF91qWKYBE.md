## 2. ACCOUNT GAME
Pass and payout numbers
- Survey of 10 FPFX firms with about 300,000 active accounts between them: 14% of traders pass the challenge and get funded, 7% of traders receive a payout, the average payout is 4% of the account value [14:09-15:09].
- The survey is "I believe from the start of the year" (2024) and was done for an unnamed industry publication [14:09, 16:09].
- His worked example: a $100,000 challenge sells for $500. 100 sold = $50,000 in. 14 get funded. 7 get paid. 7 x $4,000 = $28,000 out [15:09-16:09].
- So payouts eat 56% of fee income, before payment fees, platform fees, marketing, affiliates and staff [16:09].
- Read from his maths (my sum, not his words): half of funded traders ever get paid; the average buyer gets back about $280 per $500 fee.
- Average life of a funded account: 22 days from funded to lost. The host quotes it from an off-camera talk and the guest confirms "22 days" [1:01, 34:26].
- Trend: pass rates have gone up steadily. Payout rates and payout sizes have not gone up. More funded traders means more total payouts [17:10].
- Firms made rules easier in 2023 and early 2024. That gave high sales and bleeding profit [17:10].
- Host only, not the guest: My Forex Funds data showed funded traders in single digits percent and payouts in "decimal percentages" [13:09]. Host only: the average trader buys 5 to 10 accounts a year [39:32]. Host only: 90-something percent lose the evaluation [27:20].

How firms make money and why they fail
- Income is challenge fees. He says it looks like a sales and marketing game but is really a risk management game [5:03].
- Margins got squeezed: prices came down, rules got easier, more traders learned to game the system [5:03].
- His guess: of firms started in the last 6 months, "probably 90%" are technically insolvent. They owe more on running accounts than they hold in cash and pay old payouts from new sales [9:07]. This is a guess ("I would venture to say"), no data.
- Many firms open and close inside 60 days [10:07].
- Firms fail faster than 2 years ago because the channels they sell through are full of "toxic traders" who hunt for weak spots in rules, price feeds, execution and affiliate deals [13:09-14:09].
- MetaQuotes cut off prop firms in February to March 2024. Firms selling about $15 million of challenges a month fell to $4 million the next month. They had already spent the earlier money, so they were insolvent that day. Some vanished, some hang on [48:37].
- He thinks MetaQuotes acted on its own legal and reputation worries (it had no checks on who the firms were), not under regulator pressure [50:39].
- Healthy starting cash for a new firm: $150,000 to $300,000 at launch, after the site and tech are paid. Looser rules need more. FPFX gives firms a formula for topping up the reserve; the formula is not given [47:37-48:37].
- Size of the biggest firms: the top three or four do "between 1 and $20 million" a month in sales (garbled, likely 10 to 20 million) [53:42].
- Industry growth: 24% month over month from August to September 2024, and growth every month in 2024 [58:46].
- Gold rush period: 2020 to about mid 2023 [66:54].

How firms treat winners and manage risk
- Host asks if firms A-book some traders, B-book others, hedge, or change rules around news. Answer: "all of those things happen" [23:15].
- Firms watch the net open position of all traders added together and hedge the lopsided part [24:17].
- Hedging always uses some real money: either single accounts are A-booked (copied to a real broker) or the firm trades its own broker account using its traders' positions as a guide [24:17].
- A firm cannot net one trader against another the way a broker can, because both trade the firm's money. Example: the winner makes $10,000 and is owed about $8,000; the loser's loss brings the firm nothing. The caption says "$110,000" for the loss (garbled) [25:18].
- Fading every trader does not work. Spread, commission, swap and noise eat it. He says they tested it at length at his old brokerage: maybe 1 to 2% for "a tremendous amount of risk" [28:21].
- What they do instead: find "predictable traders" and use their trades as a signal for the firm's own trading ("alpha generation") [29:22]. In live test with FPFX's own money, aimed for release by end of 2024 [32:24-33:26].
- Trader data is used without names to train risk models. He says they were asked to sell the data and always said no [31:24].
- News trading example given to a firm: "news trading has accounted for 30% of your payouts" over 60 days. Told as an example for "firm XYZ", so the 30% may be made up. VAGUE [21:14].
- The biggest payouts usually come from traders who over-leveraged and got lucky, not from good traders [35:28].
- Higher-priced firms do not get better-behaved traders. They charge more because their audience is loyal and less price sensitive [40:32].
- Checks before funding: identity and money-laundering checks, politically exposed people are refused, the trader signs the agreement with the banned practices inside the dashboard [42:33-43:34].
- Every account and every trade a customer ever had is stored for good in the firm's CRM [44:34].

Who survives (his view)
- Does not over-leverage, does not over-trade, holds a position 1 to 3 days (swing style) [35:28].
- Short-term traders "chop themselves up" on spread and commission [35:28].
- Survivors make about 3 to 4% a month, again and again, not 10 to 50% [35:28]. Later he says 2, 3, 5% a month [38:32].
- Future: firms may prefer 1,000 good and different traders over 10,000 accounts a month, and earn from the trade data instead of fees [37:32-38:32].

Signs a firm is in trouble
- It halts payouts or blames its broker or liquidity [12:09].
- Silly promotions: buy one get one free, 75% off today. He calls them cash grabs by firms short of money [12:09].
- Prices or rules that look too good [67:54-68:56].
- One platform through one broker = single point of failure [50:39-51:40].
- A normal payout wait (for example 30 days) is not a red flag on its own [11:08].
- Payout trackers built on Rise wallets / blockchain can be faked: a firm can cycle money through a dozen wallets and back to itself. He does not believe claims of $8 million in payouts a month [52:41-54:43].
- Brokers opening prop arms are slow but have money and a name to protect. They use a separate company because prop is unregulated [59:47-65:51].

## 5. THINGS A FIRM WOULD BAN
- News trading as a coin flip: take a side into a release, retry if it fails. FPFX tracks its share of payouts even when the firm has no news rule, then advises the firm to add one [20:14-21:14].
- Two accounts on opposite sides (buy on one, sell on the other, blow one and keep the other). The host raises it; the guest says "they could", at one firm or across firms [21:14].
- Latency arbitrage and statistical arbitrage [20:14].
- "Toxic flow" (not defined, VAGUE) [20:14].
- Hunting weak spots in price feeds, trade execution or affiliate deals [14:09].
- Many traders running the same EA or following the same signal. Firms see it as concentration risk that is hard to hedge [22:15].
- Maximum leverage and "ultra high margin utilization". FPFX can build anti-gambling, anti-gaming and anti-news rules into any firm's offer [23:15].
- "Violations of the spirit of the program", even where no written rule exists. FPFX points these out so the firm can add a rule [20:14].
- Serial cheap-challenge buyers who go max size and blow up again and again (his example: a $25 challenge; 30 challenges in a month). FPFX tells firms to stop selling to them even though they bring in money [40:32-42:33].
- Careful in the evaluation, then wild once funded. The host raises it; the guest says "we have ways of filtering that out" [39:32-40:32].
- Failing identity or money-laundering checks, or being a politically exposed person: no funded account [42:33].
- Breaking any practice listed in the signed trader agreement. The full trade history is kept for good, so old behaviour can be looked up [43:34-44:34].
- Firm-side tricks the host mentions (not confirmed by the guest): slippage games and denied payouts [13:09].
- He gives no exact thresholds for any flag (no minutes around news, no margin percent, no hold time). VAGUE.

## 6. TESTING-METHOD IDEAS
- Base-rate yardstick for the prop sim: pass 14%, paid 7% of buyers (50% of funded), average payout 4% of account, funded life 22 days [14:09-15:09, 34:26]. A strategy worth an eval fee should beat all four in the sim. Caveat: CFD firms, 2024.
- Funded survival check: report days survived after funding, not only the pass rate. Most funded accounts die inside a month [34:26].
- Break-even check: at his numbers a buyer gets back about $280 per $500 fee. Compare expected first payout with the fee and resets [15:09-16:09].
- Luck check: big payouts come from one over-sized lucky trade [35:28]. Report the share of profit from the best day and best trade; fail if one day carries the result.
- News share check: report how much of a strategy's profit comes from news-release minutes, then re-run with those minutes removed. Firms add news rules when news is a big share of payouts [20:14-21:14]. Applies to release strategies such as gc_nfp and the gold 10:00 bracket.
- Cost check: short-term traders get eaten by spread and commission [35:28]. Keep the cost floor and a doubled-cost run for fast strategies.
- Size check: survivors make 3 to 4% a month [35:28]. Size so the monthly return sits near that band; treat 10%+ a month in a backtest as a sign of too much size.
- Margin check: log the highest share of allowed contracts used. Max size is a flag [23:15].
- Same-behaviour check: use the same size and rules in the eval and funded phases of the sim. A change of behaviour after funding is a flag [39:32].
- Uniqueness note: a strategy that is a common public EA or signal is more likely to be flagged [22:15]. Cannot be tested, keep as a note.
- Firm check before buying an eval (not a strategy test): payout halts, giveaway promotions, one platform only, payout claims that look too big [12:09, 50:39, 52:41].
